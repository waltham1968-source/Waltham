A digital specialist does not need to know everything. It needs enough knowledge in one defined area to provide useful first-line guidance – and to know when a human should take over.

Illustrative stories
The examples below are fictional but based on common situations. They demonstrate how the services could work and will later be improved with documented experience.

01

Which washing machine actually fits?

A family faces 47 models, opaque product codes and prices that seem impossible to compare.

The appliance specialist starts with space, usage, noise, capacity and installation rather than brand and price. It filters the range and explains the differences between three relevant options.

From catalogue to decision

The needDimensions, household size, wash frequency and important features.
Product dataModels that do not fit physically or practically are removed.
The differencesThe customer sees what each price increase actually buys.
The result

The customer moves from 47 possibilities to three understandable choices, and the retailer takes over for delivery, installation and purchase.

Price, availability and installation requirements must always be confirmed before purchase.
02

What does the bank's new rate mean?

A three-page letter announces an interest-rate change, but the customer cannot see the effect in money.

The document guide identifies the old and new rates, effective date, loan type and the bank's explanation. It translates the letter into plain language and prepares questions for the bank.

From letter to action

Key pointsAmounts, percentages, dates and terms are separated from boilerplate.
The explanationThe change is compared clearly with the current position.
The responseThe guide drafts questions and an email to the bank.
The result

The customer can approach the bank with precise questions and a stronger basis for the conversation.

Document explanation is not personal financial advice. Calculations and loan decisions must be confirmed with the bank or a qualified adviser.
03

Why did the energy bill rise?

Consumption, network charges, taxes and support appear on one bill but tell different stories.

The energy specialist compares billing periods, consumption, unit price and fixed costs. It separates what the customer can influence from network and government charges.

One bill, four explanations

ConsumptionWas more energy used than in the previous period?
PriceDid the energy price or contract type change?
Network and taxesWhich charges come from other parties?
The result

The customer can see what caused the increase and receives draft questions for the supplier.

The guide must use the actual bill and current terms and must not promise savings without current market data.
04

Three products look healthy. What differs?

The front promises protein, fibre and natural ingredients. The back requires more patience.

The nutrition specialist compares declared nutrients, ingredients, allergens and serving sizes. Answers remain grounded in documented product information.

Explanation without health promises

The same quantityValues are compared on a consistent basis.
IngredientsAllergens, sugars and relevant components are explained.
The boundaryProduct facts remain separate from personal health advice.
The result

The customer understands the differences and can make a more informed choice.

For allergies, illness, pregnancy or specific dietary needs, the user should consult a doctor or qualified nutrition professional.
