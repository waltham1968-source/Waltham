Vera Rubin could make advanced AI far cheaper

NVIDIA claims up to a tenfold reduction in cost per AI token. If the direction holds, competitive advantage may shift from access to expensive compute towards ideas, data and execution.

By Nikolai Waltham · Waltham Consulting · 23 October 2026

Wow. Do you know what Vera Rubin could come to mean?

We may be entering an era in which advanced artificial intelligence becomes not only more powerful, but dramatically cheaper to use. The name worth remembering is Vera Rubin.

Vera Rubin is NVIDIA's next integrated platform for large AI systems. It is not simply a new GPU. Processing, memory, networking, storage and software have been designed to operate as one machine. Think of it as replacing the engine, the power supply and the road network at the same time.

Three figures explain the leap

Up to 10×lower cost per token for selected advanced inference workloads compared with Blackwell.
4× fewerGPUs to train selected mixture-of-experts models, according to NVIDIA.
Up to 3.7×higher throughput than GB300 NVL72 in early, workload-specific MLPerf results.

These are large numbers, but they need to be read correctly. They do not mean that every kind of AI automatically becomes ten times better. Results depend on the model, workload, software and system configuration. The important signal is the direction: more AI work per dollar and per watt.

Named after a woman who saw the invisible

The platform is named after American astronomer Vera Rubin. In the 1970s, her observations of spiral galaxies showed that stars moved in a way visible matter could not explain. Her work provided crucial evidence for dark matter.

The name is almost poetic. Vera Rubin made an invisible force apparent through its effects. The AI platform may also have its greatest impact somewhere we do not see directly: in the price of intelligence.

The most important news may not be that AI is becoming more powerful. It is that more of that power could become economically accessible.

What does cheaper intelligence mean for business?

When cost falls, it becomes practical to let AI spend longer on a task. It can review more material, test more options, generate video and other compute-heavy content, and run agents that complete work across several steps.

  • More organisations can use advanced models in daily operations.
  • Products can include more intelligent assistance without the same cost per user.
  • AI agents can spend time researching, checking and improving their work.
  • Competition may shift from who can access the capacity to who uses it best.

This is not a reason to wait

Businesses do not need to buy a Vera Rubin system or pause development until the technology is widely available. The relevant work can begin now: organise the data, select specific workflows, build experience and decide where human oversight is necessary.

Cheaper compute does not solve an unclear problem. It simply makes both good and bad uses easier to scale.

So the strategic question is not only: How powerful will the next generation of AI become?

It is: What will we do when that power becomes affordable enough for almost everyone to use?

Sources: NVIDIA: the Rubin platform, NVIDIA: early MLPerf results and NASA: Vera Rubin and dark matter. Results and savings are workload-dependent; the platform figures are published by NVIDIA.
