AI visibility

Your business can now have a profile in ChatGPT

Business Hub lets businesses update their details in ChatGPT and see how often they are mentioned or receive clicks. We tried it with Waltham.

By Nikolai Waltham · Waltham Consulting · 16 November 2026

On 10 November, OpenAI launched business profiles in ChatGPT in beta. If you want to correct the details a potential customer sees about your business, you now have a place to do it. We have been through the first part of the process with Waltham, and our request to manage the profile was approved.

What can you do with a profile?

In Business Hub, you can search for your business and claim its profile. The options you see depend on your location and type of business. OpenAI may offer verification through your website domain, business email or phone. You also need to complete an identity check through Persona before you can publish changes and view insights.

You can then update details such as your website, business description, category and logo. You can also see aggregate figures for mentions and clicks through to your website when there is enough activity to report. At launch, Business Agents, which can answer customer questions using information supplied by the business, are available to eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses.

Make your profile specific

A good description helps a customer decide whether you can do the job. For instance, “We service and repair heat pumps in Nyborg and eastern Funen” tells the reader more than “We deliver quality solutions with the customer at the centre.” Use the same business name and web address on your profile and website, and make sure the service you mention has a page customers can find.

OpenAI says that claiming a profile does not change its visibility. ChatGPT can also draw on other sources when answering. The practical benefit is that you can correct the information you supply and start to see how often customers encounter your business in ChatGPT.
