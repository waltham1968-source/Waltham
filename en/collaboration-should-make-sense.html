Collaboration should make
sense for both sides.

On sharing, experimenting and creating visible value — and letting go well when something does not work.

We believe in collaborations where both sides can feel the value. Not only at the end, but along the way — in the energy, the learning and what we actually create together.

01

The world is round

I am fortunate to be married to the wisest woman I know — and I am also the president of her fan club. Many years ago, she said something that stayed with me:

Do not be afraid to share. The world is round — it comes back.

Since then, I have seen how often it proves true. When we share knowledge, open a door or help a good idea forward without first securing every possible advantage, something else often emerges later. Not necessarily from the same person or in the same form, but as new relationships, opportunities and experience.

02

Value should be visible along the way

Clear agreements matter. But hundreds of pages of terms cannot create trust, enthusiasm or shared direction. Nor can they save a collaboration that neither side believes in any longer.

We therefore prefer to work in small, manageable stages. We set something in motion, make the value visible and check whether both sides want to continue. If it works, we build on it. If it does not, we conclude properly, keep the learning and move on — without unnecessary conflict.

03

Sometimes we take the first risk

When we genuinely believe in a person, a concept or an opportunity, we are willing to invest some of our own time in the first exploration. This may be a rapid assessment, a sketch, a prototype or an experiment.

In those cases, the risk is ours. A missing budget should not always prevent a good idea from receiving its first chance. This is a deliberate choice made assignment by assignment — not a general promise of free consulting.

04

AI has changed the pace

Research and first drafts that once required weeks of work can now be produced in hours or minutes. This allows us to test more, learn faster and abandon what does not work before it becomes a heavy and expensive process.

The priority is therefore not to protect every experiment, but to choose the right experiments, learn from them and quickly recognise where real value appears.

05

Let go well

Not everything has to succeed. But the collaboration should be honest, the energy should travel both ways, and both sides should see a reason to continue.

Life is too short for bad collaborations. If we do not make each other better, we should let go — and find someone else with whom to create what comes next.

A simple way of working

Start small. Create visible value. Continue when it works.

And conclude properly when it does not.
