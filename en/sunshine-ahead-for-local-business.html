AI visibility and local business

Sunshine ahead? A fresh look at local business in Nyborg

After reviewing more than 400 business websites in Nyborg, Denmark, one opportunity stood out. People would have an easier time buying locally if they could see what businesses around the corner are already good at.

By Nikolai Waltham · Waltham Consulting · 20 November 2026

After more than 20 years in the polling business, I still have a soft spot for one headline in particular. Sunshine ahead. It fits what we found in Nyborg, a town in Denmark. We began by mapping the local economy and went on to review more than 400 business websites. What surprised me was how many opportunities to win work might begin with something as simple as explaining what you do.

The old wooden gate

Think of a carpenter who has been repairing old wooden gates for years. The homepage says, ‘We do all kinds of carpentry.’ If your gate is sagging, you still have to work out whether the carpenter takes on that kind of job.

Now imagine the same page with a photo of a gate, a few lines explaining how it was fixed and a clear note on where the carpenter works. You have something concrete to go on. If the job goes well, you’ll also have a number to call the next time something sticks or needs fixing.

What can AI find?

The same patterns turned up repeatedly in our review. Services were described in broad terms, contact details and service areas were hard to find, or specialist skills were tucked away in an old news post or a PDF. A website can look polished and still leave customers’ most important questions unanswered.

Our hypothesis is that specific information gives an AI service a better basis for mentioning a business when someone asks, ‘Who can repair an old wooden gate near Nyborg?’ The same example also helps the person deciding whom to call.

What we want to measure next

I’d like to try this with a small group of local businesses. Each would pick two kinds of work it wants more of and describe them with an example, a service area and an easy way to get in touch. We could then repeat the same local queries to see which businesses AI services mention and track whether those businesses receive more relevant enquiries, requests for quotes and bookings.

That’s what I find encouraging. Some of what we’re looking for is already here. Make it easier to find the right person, and one job can become a relationship worth having the next time you need help.
