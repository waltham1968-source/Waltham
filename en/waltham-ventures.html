Time to market is a strategic advantage

It can take minutes to become visible in the market. It still takes judgement to build something customers will pay for.

New technology makes it possible to launch a first landing page, offer, lead channel or market test almost immediately — and adapt continuously to real signals. This reverses the development model: less prolonged planning, faster contact with reality.

A fast launch is not the same as a proven market. The objective is to learn earlier, before time and capital are committed to the wrong solution.

The experience advantage

The over-50s are not a residual group. They may be the next wave of venture builders.

OECD data show that employment among 55–64-year-olds across member countries rose from 47.7 per cent in 2004 to 66.4 per cent in 2024. Experienced people are more active in the labour market than before — while AI lowers the threshold for turning knowledge into digital solutions.

The value is not that experience replaces technical skill. It lies in the combination: relationships, situational understanding, credibility and pattern recognition can now work with tools that make research, production and launch far more accessible.

Collaboration model in development

Experience should not retire simply because technology changes.

Many experienced, independent people have strong networks, sound commercial judgement and a continuing desire to create. Waltham wants to explore a model where that experience meets shared, rapid methods for research, market testing, production and launch.

This should not be a loose network or a movement built on grand claims. It should be a curated working community around concrete opportunities, clear roles and fair economics.

The partner brings

  • Sector insight and practical experience
  • A relevant network and access to the market
  • An idea, customer problem or concrete opportunity

Waltham brings

  • Rapid research and market verification
  • Positioning, digital production and first launch
  • A reusable method for learning quickly

Before we build

  • Agree ownership, roles, costs and potential revenue
  • Define the smallest test and a clear stopping rule
  • Share the learning — including when the test tells us to stop
Test small. Learn fast. Build further when the market responds. Let go properly when it does not.

The objective is not merely to survive an AI-shaped future. It is to give experienced people a practical way to remain active, relevant and value-creating within it.
