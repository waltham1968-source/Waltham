A great website is a seaworthy yacht.

But it still needs a course and a way to be found. A maritime perspective on visibility, direction and customer understanding.

By Nikolai Waltham

Launched.
But still out of sight.

Imagine building a fine yacht. Sound hull, good gear, ready for sea. Then you launch her into a vast stretch of open water. Not another vessel in sight. Nobody knows where you are, or what you have to offer.

Launching a new website can feel much the same. The design looks right. The words are in place. Everything works. Yet very few enquiries arrive.

A website gives customers somewhere to arrive. It does not automatically tell the market you exist. And once someone finds you, they still need to understand who you can help and why your offer deserves a closer look.

A sound yacht makes the voyage possible.
A clear course gives it purpose.

The sea is vast.
But it can be read.

From shore, the sea can look like one restless expanse. An old hand sees wind, currents and bearings. Markets have their signals too. We can learn to read them and understand how they fit together.

The wind: what customers need

We cannot command the wind. We can trim the sails. Customer questions, enquiries and experiences reveal the problems our business should make easier to solve.

The current: how customers find you

When a customer asks an AI assistant to find and compare suppliers, your business needs to make sense in the sources it can consult. The route customers take to you can shift while your website stays exactly where it is.

The bearings: what others can trust

A clear offer, concrete examples and credible independent mentions give customers their bearings. Grand claims about your own brilliance are of little use if the story cannot be checked.

AI can help us make sense of the signals.
People choose the destination and keep the helm.

Set your course.
Make your business clear.

Before adding another campaign or another tool, check whether customers can understand your offer and see a clear next step.

  1. Choose whom you want to reach

    Which customers do you want to help, and what are they trying to solve? Use the words they use.

  2. Make the offer concrete

    Explain what you do, for whom and where. Show examples that make the value easier to judge.

  3. Give the story evidence

    Use case studies, customer experiences and relevant external sources to support what you say.

  4. Check whether you are understood

    Try real customer questions in search engines and AI assistants. Check how your business is described and which sources support the answer.

  5. Measure what creates headway

    Track relevant enquiries, their quality and the path from interest to conversation. Visitor numbers alone cannot tell you whether the course is right.

AI answers vary with the question, sources and timing. Clear information and evidence strengthen the basis for discovery and consideration, but do not guarantee a recommendation.

You have built a fine yacht.
Can the right people find her?
