About

A Norwegian who found
his home at sea and in Denmark.

I grew up with an instinct to build things. Not out of ambition in the classical sense—but out of genuine curiosity about how things work, what happens when you try something new, and what becomes possible when you simply begin.

Over the past three decades I've started companies, filed patents, managed properties, and crossed oceans. I've built things that worked and things that didn't. The failures taught me more.

Today I live and work in Nyborg on the Danish island of Funen. I manage commercial properties, explore the emerging world of AI, and spend as much time as possible on the water. I believe that good work happens when you stay curious, stay humble, and stay close to the people you serve.

I was born in Norway, but Denmark shaped me. And the sea—the North Sea, the Atlantic, the Belt—continues to educate me in ways no classroom ever could.

Entrepreneurship

A life of building,
one chapter at a time.

Age 16

First Business — Pancakes

It started with a griddle, some batter and a market stall. At sixteen I sold pancakes and learned something nobody teaches in business school: that customers decide everything. That lesson has never left me.

1990s

First Companies

A series of early ventures exploring what it meant to build something from scratch—services, trade, early digital. Some grew. Some didn't. Each one taught me something the previous one couldn't.

2000s

InFact — Business Intelligence

Founded InFact with the conviction that better information leads to better decisions. Developed products and frameworks to help businesses understand their own data. A long, patient project.

2000s–2010s

Patent Development

Ideas that wanted to become inventions. Working through the patent process taught me the discipline of precision—how to describe an idea so completely and clearly that it becomes defensible.

2015–present

Property Administration

Managing Lindholm Business Center in Nyborg and AltiPark Højlager in Svendborg on behalf of HC Ejendomme. An exercise in trust, relationships and the quiet satisfaction of spaces that work well for the people inside them.

2023–present

AI Exploration & Automation

The emergence of practical AI feels like the early days of the internet—full of noise, but genuinely transformative underneath. I'm building tools, automating workflows and thinking hard about what this means for small businesses and property management.

Sailing

The sea is the best
teacher I've had.

I've been sailing for as long as I can remember, and the sea has a way of cutting through everything unnecessary. Out there, there's no room for excuses, ego or wishful thinking. You read the conditions, make a decision and live with it.

That discipline—honest assessment, decisive action, continuous adjustment—is something I carry back to shore with me every time.

Sailing isn't a hobby for me. It's a philosophy with salt in it.

Philosophy

A few things I
actually believe.

Small adjustments can create great movements.

The biggest changes in a boat's course come from the smallest movements of the rudder. Business and life work the same way. Consistency of small, correct decisions compounds into transformation.

Curiosity is not a personality trait. It's a discipline.

Staying genuinely curious requires effort, especially as you accumulate experience. The temptation to rely on what you already know is enormous. Resisting it is the work.

Facts are friendly. They tell you where you actually are.

The truth about a situation—however uncomfortable—is always more useful than a comfortable story. This is why I built InFact. Honest data is the starting point for good decisions.

The best infrastructure is invisible.

A building that works perfectly, a system that runs silently, a process that nobody notices—these are the highest achievements. Excellence in the background enables excellence up front.
