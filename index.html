I have never been especially interested in doing things simply because that is how they are usually done.

Today my work moves between commercial property, practical problem-solving and new tools. I manage places where businesses need to function, explore ideas that may become useful, and use AI where it genuinely improves the work.

The tool is rarely the interesting part. The interesting part is seeing the real problem, asking a better question and finding the small adjustment that changes the course.

A personal operating system

Not quite standard.
That is often useful.

I have been told that I am not entirely conventional. I take that as a compliment. These are not rules for everyone. They are patterns that have followed me through companies, inventions, buildings and oceans.

01

Look at what is actually there.

Facts are friendly. A clear view of the situation is more useful than a comfortable explanation.

02

Begin before everything is perfect.

Many useful things only reveal themselves after you start. Movement creates information.

03

Find the smallest useful adjustment.

A small movement of the rudder can change the destination. Business and life often work the same way.

04

Build for use, not applause.

The best systems are often nearly invisible. They simply make it easier for people to do good work.

05

Adjust the course, not the weather.

Conditions change. Complaining about them uses energy that is better spent making the next decision.

Selected stories

A life of trying,
one problem at a time.

Not a timeline. A few moments that explain how I learned to work.

The first lesson · Age 16

A griddle, some batter and a market stall.

My first business was selling pancakes. It was simple enough to expose the essentials: make something, meet the customer and learn immediately whether it works. Customers decide. That lesson has stayed with me.

Principle: Reality gives faster feedback than theory.

InFact · Business intelligence

Better decisions begin with an honest picture.

InFact grew from a conviction that decisions should be grounded in facts rather than assumptions. The work was not about producing more data. It was about making the right information useful.

Principle: Facts are friendly.

Patent development · Precision

An idea becomes stronger when it can be explained clearly.

Working through patent development taught me to remove vagueness. If an idea cannot be described precisely, it is not yet ready to be defended—or built.

Principle: Clarity is part of the invention.

Property · Daily work

The best infrastructure disappears into the day.

At Lindholm Business Center and AltiPark Højlager, the work is practical and human: listen, notice friction and make the place function. When it works well, most people should not have to think about it.

Principle: Quiet systems enable visible work.

At sea

The sea is the best
teacher I have had.

Out there, there is no room for wishful thinking. You read the conditions, make a decision and live with it.

I have crossed the Atlantic, crossed the North Sea, raced offshore and sailed single-handed around Funen in Silverrudder. The sea keeps teaching the same quiet discipline: assess honestly, act decisively and adjust continuously.

“When the wind changes, you do not get offended. You adjust your course.”

About

Norwegian by birth.
Danish by home.
Educated by the sea.

I have spent more than three decades starting companies, developing ideas, managing property and learning from what did—and did not—work.

Today I live in Nyborg on the Danish island of Funen. My work is still driven by the same instinct I had when I began: understand how something works, question what everyone assumes, and see whether there is a more useful way forward.

This site is not intended to be a complete CV. It is a growing collection of projects, stories, methods and observations from work and life.
