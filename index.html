The idea

AI is new.
The questions aren't.

For more than 25 years, I have been fascinated by one question: how can knowledge, decisions and repetitive work be turned into systems that work better?

At InFact, that meant automated telephone systems capable of conducting hundreds of conversations at once. Today, it means AI assistants, knowledge systems and workflows. The technology has changed dramatically. The way I approach a problem has not.

I am not the person you hire to write every line of code. I work best before that: understanding the real problem, seeing connections across disciplines, challenging assumptions and deciding what is worth building. Then I bring in people who are technically better than me to make it real.

The goal is not technology for its own sake. It is to remove unnecessary work, protect human attention and create more time for what actually matters.

Problems I enjoyed solving

The path was never straight.
The pattern was.

Early 1980s · Age 16

Learning through my hands

I began assembling and repairing bicycles in an Oslo bike shop. Mechanics taught me a lasting lesson: if I do not understand a system, I can take it apart, learn it and put it back together. Renovation jobs, engines and boatbuilding added more tools to the same practical education.

Early ventures

Reklamevask — starting with borrowed tools

A friend and I borrowed a van and a pressure washer, then sold clean façades to shops and cafés. Almost no capital, one clear observation and direct action. An early lesson in making something useful from what was already available.

1990s

Ski- og Sykkelstasjonen

We wanted the best equipment for the sports we loved, so we built a shop. With enthusiasm instead of capital, we convinced a supplier to fill it with bikes on consignment and worked with a ski factory to create our own wide, soft telemark skis. Once the concept worked, I handed the business to my partners and looked for the next challenge.

1990s

SmartSense — inventing the application

I connected an East German gas sensor with Norway's changing indoor-smoking rules. The engineers created an electronic nose that detected smoke and activated an electrostatic filter. We sold the first systems. I did not invent the sensor; I saw a new use for it.

Late 1990s

Old boats, long passages

I bought an old sailboat unseen in Malta, made the deal on arrival and sailed it home via the Azores. Later I revived a stripped, confiscated boat on the Azores with whatever parts I could find, then sailed it through the English Channel and North Sea. Incomplete systems under pressure have a way of clarifying what matters.

2000–2024

InFact — automation with a purpose

InFact began with a wish for freedom: a company I could run from anywhere with an internet connection. Automated telephone systems eventually handled hundreds of simultaneous conversations. Polls became headlines, headlines created attention, and attention led to the next customer—a system that produced both insight and momentum.

2022–present

Generative AI — the next tool

I put ChatGPT to practical use soon after it became public: first in board work, later in the sale of my company, and now across analysis, websites, property, documentation and new ventures. AI is not a new identity. It is the natural continuation of a lifetime spent asking how systems can carry more of the repetitive work.

The operator, not the driver

Trim the system.
Then let it work.

On a passage lasting ten or fourteen days, the goal is not to fight the helm every minute. You invest time in balancing the sails, the boat and the windvane until the boat can sail herself.

Then your role changes. You become the operator rather than the driver: watching the conditions, protecting the system and making small corrections when the weather changes.

I recognise the same principle in a good company and a well-designed AI workflow. The initial thinking matters. Balance matters. And human judgment still decides when to trust the system and when to intervene.

Principles

What all the stories
have taught me.

Time is the only resource we never get more of.

I have never measured an opportunity only by the money it might produce. I also count the future emails, calls, meetings and obligations. A good system does not merely save money. It returns attention and time to people.

Build the autopilot. Keep the judgment.

The goal is not to remove people from the process. It is to remove people from unnecessary work. Machines can carry the repetition; humans should keep responsibility, context and the ability to intervene.

Make soup from a nail.

Start with what you have. Make it useful. Invite others in. Let one result create the next. Many of my companies began with little more than a practical observation, borrowed equipment and enough enthusiasm to start the wheel turning.

Work smarter. Create more. Spend more days in the sun.

Freedom is not about doing nothing. It is having room to remain curious, build something, help someone, spend time with family, go sailing and keep playing for as long as life allows.
